Is the market becoming more or less willing to bear credit risk, and where in the quality spectrum is stress appearing? Broad public credit-market data only — ICE BofA OAS indices via FRED. Not yet covered: single-name CDS, TRACE, DTCC, lending standards, issuer-specific bonds.
Spreads
Option-adjusted spread (OAS) over a Treasury spot curve, in basis points. Wider spreads generally indicate greater compensation demanded for credit and liquidity risk; narrower means tightening. Percentiles are computed over each series' actual available FRED history (window shown per card) — these ICE BofA indices currently only carry ~3 years of public history, not decades.
Quality Curve
The credit-market equivalent of a yield curve: how much additional spread the market demands as credit quality falls from investment grade (IG) down to CCC & lower.

Risk Dispersion calc
Heimdall-calculated spreads between rating tiers, both sides aligned to the same observation date before subtracting (never forward-filled). A wider CCC−BBB gap indicates stronger differentiation between lower-quality and investment-grade borrowers.
